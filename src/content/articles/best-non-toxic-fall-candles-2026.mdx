## Why Conventional Candles Are a Problem

Most candles on the shelf — including the expensive "luxury" ones — are made from paraffin wax. Paraffin is a petroleum byproduct. When you burn it, you're releasing the same chemicals found in diesel exhaust: benzene, toluene, and formaldehyde.

That's not alarmist — it's chemistry. The American Lung Association has documented indoor air quality issues from burning paraffin candles, and the EPA lists several paraffin combustion byproducts as known carcinogens.

But the wax is only half the problem.

### The Fragrance Loophole

When a label says "fragrance," it can legally hide hundreds of undisclosed chemicals — and none of them have to be listed. The term "fragrance" is protected as a trade secret in the U.S., which means companies can use phthalates (known endocrine disruptors), synthetic musks (which accumulate in human tissue), and benzene derivatives without ever putting them on the label.

"Fall Spice" or "Pumpkin Latte" sound natural. They're not. They're lab-synthesized fragrance oils designed to mimic natural scents at a fraction of the cost — and the chemicals used to create them have been linked to hormone disruption, asthma exacerbation, and allergic reactions.

**The three things to avoid:**
1. **Paraffin wax** — petroleum-derived, releases carcinogens when burned
2. **"Fragrance" or "parfum"** on the label with no ingredient disclosure
3. **Lead-core wicks** (banned in the U.S. since 2003, but still found in some imports)

## Clean Wax Types: What Actually Burns Clean

| Wax Type | Source | Burn Time | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Beeswax** | Natural, from honeybees | Longest (up to 2x soy) | Naturally purifies air by releasing negative ions. Warm, honey-like scent. |
| **Soy Wax** | Soybean oil | Moderate (40-50 hrs per 8 oz) | Clean-burning, renewable, holds fragrance well. Burns cooler than paraffin. |
| **Coconut Wax** | Coconut oil | Long, slow burn | Often blended with soy. Excellent scent throw with natural oils. |
| **Paraffin Wax** | Petroleum | Short, sooty | Avoid. Releases toluene, benzene, formaldehyde. |

The best non-toxic candles use a blend of two or more clean waxes — soy + coconut is the most common and effective combination. Beeswax is the gold standard for purity but has a naturally sweet scent that doesn't blend with every fragrance profile.
