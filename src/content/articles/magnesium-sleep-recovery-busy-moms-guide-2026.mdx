## Are You Magnesium Deficient? The Signs Active Moms Miss

Magnesium deficiency doesn't announce itself with a single obvious symptom. Instead, it shows up as a constellation of issues that are easy to blame on "being busy" or "getting older":

| Symptom | Why Magnesium Matters |
|---|---|
| **Muscle cramps at night** | Magnesium regulates muscle contraction; low levels cause involuntary spasms |
| **Can't fall asleep despite exhaustion** | Magnesium activates GABA receptors, the brain's "calm down" signal |
| **Wake up feeling unrested** | Without adequate magnesium, deep sleep (slow-wave) is reduced |
| **Post-workout soreness that lingers** | Magnesium reduces inflammation and helps clear lactate |
| **Anxiety or racing thoughts at bedtime** | Magnesium regulates cortisol and the HPA stress axis |
| **Eye twitching or muscle spasms** | Classic early sign of magnesium depletion |
| **Chocolate cravings** | Chocolate is high in magnesium; cravings can signal deficiency |

If you checked three or more of these, magnesium supplementation is worth a serious look. The good news: it's one of the safest, most well-studied supplements available — and results often show within the first week.

## Magnesium Bisglycinate vs. Magnesium Malate: Which One Do You Need?

Not all magnesium is created equal. The form matters enormously — both for what it does in your body and whether it'll send you running to the bathroom (magnesium citrate, I'm looking at you).

| Form | Best For | Absorption | GI Side Effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Magnesium Bisglycinate** | Sleep, anxiety, muscle relaxation | Excellent | Very low |
| **Magnesium Malate** | Daytime energy, fibromyalgia, muscle pain | Good | Low |
| **Magnesium Citrate** | Constipation | Moderate | **High** (laxative effect) |
| **Magnesium Oxide** | Budget option | Poor (4% absorption) | Moderate |

**For sleep and recovery, magnesium bisglycinate is the gold standard.** The glycine molecule it's bound to is itself a calming neurotransmitter — so you get a dual effect: the magnesium relaxes your muscles, and the glycine calms your brain. This is the form I recommend for nightly use.

Magnesium malate is better suited for daytime — the malic acid component supports cellular energy production (the Krebs cycle). Some women take malate in the morning and bisglycinate at night, but for most busy moms, keeping it simple with a single high-quality bisglycinate supplement at bedtime is the sweet spot.
