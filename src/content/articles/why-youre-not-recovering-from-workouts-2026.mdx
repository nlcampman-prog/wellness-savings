## The Problem Isn't Your Workout. It's Everything Else.

Here's the uncomfortable truth almost nobody wants to hear: your workout is the *stimulus*, not the result. The result happens between workouts — while you sleep, while you eat, while you rest. If you're not recovering, you're not growing. Period.

Coaches used to tell me to "just push harder." That was terrible advice. I've seen women run themselves into the ground trying to out-train a poor recovery foundation. So let's look at the four real reasons busy women don't recover — and what to actually do about each one.

## Reason #1: You're Running on a Sleep Debt You Can't Out-Exercise

I know. Nobody wants to hear "sleep more" when they're already exhausted. But here's the thing — sleep is when your body actually repairs muscle tissue, clears out metabolic waste, and regulates the hormones that control hunger, stress, and recovery.

When you're a mom, "sleep more" can feel like a punchline. I get it. I've got five kids. There were years I thought 5.5 hours was normal.

But the science is clear: short sleep raises cortisol, tanks growth hormone, and leaves you sore and inflamed regardless of what you do in the gym. If you've ever wondered why you're sore for days after a workout that used to be easy, sleep debt is very often the culprit. The non-negotiable fix isn't "sleep 10 hours" (ain't gonna happen). It's protecting *one* thing: prioritize your sleep window, drop the phone an hour before bed, and seriously consider a magnesium supplement. I'll come back to that.

## Reason #2: You're Not Eating Enough Protein (Especially After 35)

I have an unpopular opinion, and I'm going to say it anyway: most women I talk to are not eating nearly enough protein to actually recover from the exercise they're doing.

Here's the reality. When you work out, you're literally tearing muscle fibers. To rebuild them stronger, your body needs amino acids — the building blocks. If you're not eating enough protein, your body can't repair the damage. You stay sore, you stay inflamed, and you stop seeing results.

## Reason #3: Stress and Cortisol Are Eating Your Recovery

Here's the one nobody talks about when they're selling you a workout program: chronic stress.

Here's what happens. When you're constantly stressed — work deadlines, kids, bills, the endless mental load of being a mom — your body pumps out cortisol. A little cortisol after a workout is normal and fine. But *chronic* high cortisol keeps you in a state of fight-or-flight. It interferes with muscle repair, promotes belly fat storage, and keeps you from getting the deep sleep your body needs to recover.

Sound familiar? If you've been working out consistently and yet feel like you're getting softer, sleepier, and more stressed — cortisol is a huge part of that picture. The fix isn't to "relax more" — that's useless advice. It's to actively build stress management into your recovery: a walk, breathing, actually resting on your rest days, and lowering the noise. There are two ways people handle soreness. Either they push straight through it (that's me before I learned better), or they do nothing at all. Both are wrong.

The sweet spot is *active recovery* — gentle movement that increases blood flow without adding more stress. Think walking, easy cycling, mobility work, foam rolling, light stretching. This clears out metabolic waste, delivers nutrients to recovering muscles, and honestly just makes you feel human again.
