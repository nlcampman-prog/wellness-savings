## First: No, It Won't Make You Bulky

Let's get this out of the way, because it's the thing I hear most and it's rooted in a misunderstanding.

Creatine does not make women "bulky." It never did. What creates bulk is years of heavy resistance training *combined* with a calorie surplus — and even then, women simply don't have the testosterone to build big muscle the way men do.

What creatine actually does is help your muscles hold a bit more water (intramuscularly, inside the muscle cell — not puffy water retention) so they can work a little harder and recover a little faster. The result isn't "bulky." The result is *toned*, *firm*, and functionally stronger. It's the difference between looking soft and looking defined, especially when you pair it with strength training.

## What Creatine Actually Does (The 30-Second Science)

Creatine is made up of three amino acids — arginine, glycine, and methionine — and your body stores about 95% of it in your muscles and the rest in your brain and organs.

Here's the simplest way to understand it: your body's energy currency is called ATP. When you sprint, lift, or push hard, you burn through ATP fast. Creatine helps you *recycle* ATP quickly, so you can contract harder and recover faster between efforts.

That's why it's so good for strength and power. But here's the part most people don't realize: **your brain also runs on ATP.** And as you age, both your muscle and your brain become less efficient at producing it. That's a big deal — and it's exactly why the over-35 crowd gets outsized benefits.

## The Perimenopause Angle Nobody Is Talking About

I want to be really honest with you about what happens around 35-40, because I lived it and I see it in so many of you.

Your hormones start shifting. Estrogen, a huge player in muscle, bone, and brain health, starts to decline. And with that decline comes a cascade of things women quietly suffer through:

- **Muscle loss.** After about 35, you naturally lose 3-8% of your muscle mass per decade, and that accelerates during perimenopause and especially after menopause. Less muscle means a slower metabolism, more joint strain, and a higher risk of falls and fractures later.
- **Bone density loss.** Estrogen protects your bones. As it drops, your risk of osteopenia and osteoporosis climbs.
- **Brain fog.** The "where did I park the car," "what was I just about to say" fog that gets blamed on being tired, being busy, or being a mom. It's real, and it's tied to energy and hormonal changes.

Here's what I wish someone had told me: creatine touches *all three* of those. It's not a magic pill, but it's one of the few supplements that genuinely supports muscle, bone, and brain at the same time. And it's ridiculously well studied.

## 2. It's a Genuine Support for Brain Fog

I'm a speech-language pathologist by training (yes, I still use that brain every single day), so I tend to think about cognition a lot. And brain fog — that scattered, forgetful, "I walked into this room and forgot why" feeling — is one of the most common complaints I hear from women in their late 30s and 40s.

Here's the connection: your brain uses a surprising amount of energy, and it relies on quick ATP regeneration to think clearly, focus, and remember. As you age and sleep gets worse (hello, perimenopause insomnia and mom life), your brain's energy supply takes a hit.

Creatine helps your brain regenerate ATP more efficiently — which is why a growing body of research shows it can support cognitive performance, especially during and after sleep deprivation. It won't fix the sleep deprivation itself, but it can help your brain function better *despite* it.

## 3. It Supports Muscle and Bone During the Hormonal Shift

This is the part I care about most for myself and for the women reading this.

As estrogen declines, both muscle and bone density are at risk. Creatine, especially when combined with resistance training, is one of the better-studied ways to support muscle mass. And while creatine isn't "a bone supplement," preserving muscle and staying strong has a real protective effect — strong muscles support strong bones, and staying capable and active is the single best thing you can do for long-term bone health.

The takeaway isn't complicated: the women who do the best through the hormonal transition are the ones who stay strong. Creatine is a tool that makes staying strong easier and more effective.

## 4. Honest Notes on the Other Stuff

Two things I want to flag honestly, because I know you'll wonder.

**Water retention:** In the first couple of weeks, creatine can cause a little weight gain from water held in your muscles. This is normal, it's in the muscle (not puffiness), and it usually settles. It's a sign it's working, not a reason to stop.

**Dosing:** You don't need the old-school "loading phase" (a week of 20g that makes you feel blah). For most women, a simple daily dose of **3-5g of creatine monohydrate** is plenty. Consistency matters more than timing — just take it daily, whenever is easy. I mix mine into my morning smoothie or just plain water.

**Which type:** Creatine monohydrate is the gold standard — it's the most studied, the most effective, and the cheapest. Ignore the fancy "buffered" or "patented" versions marketing to you.
