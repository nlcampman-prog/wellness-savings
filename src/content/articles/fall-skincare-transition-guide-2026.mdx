## The Physiology of Autumn Skin: Why Your Summer Routine is Drying You Out

To understand why your skin feels dry, we have to look at the science of the skin barrier, specifically the stratum corneum (the outermost layer of your skin).

During the summer, high outdoor humidity acts as a natural humectant, pulling moisture into your skin. 

To cope with heat and sweat, your summer routine likely focused on lightweight, oil-free hydration, deep-cleansing gel face washes to prevent clogged pores, and mineral SPF to block intense UV rays.

But when autumn arrives, three major environmental shifts occur simultaneously:

1.  **Drop in Humidity:** The air becomes significantly drier. Through a process called **Transepidermal Water Loss (TEWL)**, moisture is literally evaporated from your skin cells into the dry air.
2.  **Indoor Heating Activation:** Turning on your home and office heaters strips the remaining ambient moisture from the indoor air, exacerbating skin dehydration.
3.  **Blood Vessel Constriction:** Cold winds cause blood vessels in the skin to constrict, reducing nutrient and oxygen delivery to the skin surface, leading to a dull, sluggish complexion.

If you continue using harsh summer foaming washes and lightweight gels, you will strip away your skin's natural sebum (protective oils), leading to microscopic tears, redness, flaking, and heightened sensitivity.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Do I still need to wear sunscreen in the fall?
Yes, absolutely! While UVB rays (which cause sunburn) are weaker in the fall, **UVA rays (which cause premature aging, wrinkles, and cellular damage) remain at the exact same strength all year round**. 

Make sure to apply a clean, hydrating sunscreen every single morning as the final step of your skincare routine.

### My skin feels dry but I'm still breaking out. Why?
When your skin barrier is dry and stripped of oil, it panics and enters a state of **reactive seborrhea**—meaning it physically overproduces sebum to compensate for the dryness. This excess oil, mixed with dry, dead skin cells that aren't shedding properly, is a recipe for stubborn autumn breakouts. Focus on barrier repair, not harsh acne washes!
