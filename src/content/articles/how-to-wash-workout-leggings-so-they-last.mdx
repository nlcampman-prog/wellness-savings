## Why "clean" gym clothes still smell

Sweat is mostly water. The smell is bacteria plus leftover skin oils in a synthetic knit that does not breathe like cotton.

If you:

- leave a damp pair in a gym bag overnight,
- wash on warm with regular detergent only,
- add fabric softener or dryer sheets,
- then tumble dry hot,

you coat the fibers. The next workout's sweat sits on that film. You get the sour smell people swear is "the brand." It is a biofilm in a polyester-nylon blend.

**Fix the wash before you buy a new pair.** I have brought "dead" leggings back with one strip-wash (below) more than once.

## The actual wash rules (the short list I tape to the laundry room in my head)

1. **Turn them inside out.** Pills and snags happen on the outside. Odor lives on the inside.
2. **Cold water, gentle cycle.** Heat sets odor and cooks elastane.
3. **Sport detergent or a small scoop of regular — never fabric softener.** Softener is wax on stretch fibers. It kills wicking. That is not a rumor. That is the point of the product.
4. **Skip dryer sheets.** Same problem, airborne.
5. **Hang dry.** Over a door, a rack, the shower rod. Not the steering wheel in a closed car for three days if you can help it — mildew is a different smell and a worse one.
6. **Wash sooner than you want to.** A pile of damp leggings is a petri dish. If I cannot wash tonight, I hang them open until I can.

That is the whole religion. Everything else is damage control.

## The strip-wash for the pair that already stinks

Once. Not every week.

Fill a sink or tub with warm — not boiling — water. Add a tablespoon of baking soda and a small splash of white vinegar *or* a sport-wash like you would use on a yoga mat smell. Soak 30 minutes. Rinse. Wash cold as usual. Hang dry.

If they still smell, the elastane is tired or the knit is holding oil you will not get out. Retire them to house-only. Do not take a sour pair to Costco and call it a personality.

Do not mix vinegar and bleach. Ever. This is a sink soak, not a chemistry set.

## What I do not do (anymore)

- **Hot wash "to kill bacteria."** Cold plus detergent plus not leaving them wet does the job. Heat kills the stretch.
- **One giant mixed load with towels.** Towels are sandpaper. Leggings pill. Wash activewear with activewear, or at least not with bath towels and jeans.
- **Leaving them in the washer overnight.** Sour washer smell transfers. Run the load or pull them into a hang.

## FAQ

### Can I put a mesh bag in the dryer on low?
You can. I still hang. "Low" in a family dryer that just did towels is not actually low. If you must machine-dry, low, short, pull them still slightly damp, finish on a hanger.

### Why do black leggings fade?
Hot water, hot dryer, and detergent dumped on the fabric. Inside out, cold, hang. Fade is not only sun.

### Do I need a special sport detergent?
It helps for chronic odor. It is not required if you wash soon, skip softener, and hang. I use sport wash when a pair has already gone sour.

### How long should a pair last?
With this care, a year of real mom wear is normal. Six weeks is a wash problem or a fabric that was never squat-proof. Test coverage with the [bathroom squat method](/articles/how-to-tell-if-leggings-are-squat-proof/) before you blame laundry.

## The laundry-room version

Cold. Inside out. No softener. Hang. Wash before they ferment in a bag. Strip-soak once if they already smell. Buy dark **Athleta**, **CRZ YOGA**, or **Old Navy PowerPress** depending on budget and job — then treat all three the same in the wash.

That is how squat-proof lasts longer than the unboxing photo.
