## What Makes a Great Educational Toy?

Before we dive into picks, a quick framework. A good educational toy for ages 2–5 should do at least three of these:

1. **Encourage open-ended play.** There's no single "right" way to use it. Blocks can be castles or rockets. A play kitchen can be a restaurant or a science lab.
2. **Build a specific skill.** Fine motor, pattern recognition, cause and effect, counting — ideally more than one skill per toy.
3. **Grow with the child.** A toy a 2-year-old can stack should also be something a 5-year-old can build complex structures with.
4. **Be genuinely fun.** If it feels like homework, they won't touch it. The learning has to be invisible.

## Screen-Free Summer: A Realistic Approach

I'm not anti-screen. With five kids, screens have saved my sanity more times than I can count. But summer 2026, we're trying something different: **screens by design, not by default.**

Here's what's working:

1. **Morning toy rotation:** I set out 2–3 educational toys (puzzles, flashcards, building sets) before breakfast. They're there, visible, and get used before the iPad temptation kicks in.
2. **The play tent as a screen-free zone:** Books, stuffed animals, and quiet toys only inside the TinyLand tent. It naturally draws kids away from screens without a "no screens" battle.
3. **Wonder Math as the bridge:** When screen time happens, make it count. Wonder Math is genuinely educational, and 20 minutes feels like a win rather than a concession.
4. **Outdoor toy integration:** Take the Brightzy flashcards outside. Do letter sounds in the backyard. Learning doesn't need to happen at a desk.

## Where to Save on Educational Toys

Quality educational toys aren't cheap — but they don't have to break the budget either. Here's what I've learned:

- **Rotate, don't accumulate.** You don't need 50 educational toys. 5–7 high-quality items in rotation beats a playroom full of things they ignore.
- **Buy open-ended.** A play tent, a set of blocks, a play kitchen — these have years of use across multiple ages. A single-purpose electronic toy may last a season.
- **Look for sales and bundles.** TinyLand and Brightzy both offer bundles that bring down the per-item cost. BCP frequently runs deals on larger playsets.
