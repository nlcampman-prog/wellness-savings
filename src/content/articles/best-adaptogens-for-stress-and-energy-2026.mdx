## What Are Adaptogens, Actually?

Adaptogens are herbs and mushrooms that help your body regulate its stress response system — the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal (HPA) axis. When you're under chronic stress (hello, parenting), your HPA axis gets stuck in "on" mode: cortisol stays elevated, your nervous system stays ramped, and over time you burn out.

Adaptogens work by:
- **Normalizing cortisol** — bringing it down when it's too high, supporting it when it's too low
- **Modulating neurotransmitters** — supporting dopamine, serotonin, and GABA balance
- **Protecting cells from oxidative stress** — the cellular damage that chronic stress accelerates

They're not stimulants (they won't give you a jolt like caffeine) and they're not sedatives (they won't knock you out like melatonin). They help restore balance — and that's exactly what the fall transition demands.

## What to Expect Week by Week

Adaptogens aren't caffeine. You won't feel them in 20 minutes. Here's a realistic timeline:

| Week | What You'll Notice |

| **Week 1** | Subtle. Maybe slightly easier to fall asleep, slightly less reactive to kid chaos. |

The key: consistency. Adaptogens work by gradually shifting your stress-response baseline, not by acute intervention. Take them daily, same time, and give them a full month before you evaluate.

## A Note on Safety

Adaptogens are generally safe, but they're not for everyone:
- **Ashwagandha** can interact with thyroid medications and may not be appropriate during pregnancy — check with your provider.
- **Rhodiola** can be slightly stimulating — take it before 2 p.m. to avoid sleep disruption.
- **Reishi** can have mild blood-thinning effects — discontinue before surgery.

As always, talk to your healthcare provider before starting any new supplement, especially if you're pregnant, nursing, or on medication. This is general information, not medical advice.
