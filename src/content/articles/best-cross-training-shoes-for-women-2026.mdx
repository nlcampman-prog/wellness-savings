## Why You Can't Wear Running Shoes for Cross-Training

Before we look at the shoes, let's look at the science of why running shoes are actually working against you in a functional workout.

Running shoes are built with thick, compressible foam midsoles and a high heel-to-toe drop (usually 8mm to 12mm). This is designed to absorb impact as your heel strikes the ground and propel you forward. 

Cross-training shoes, on the other hand, feature:
- **Low heel-to-toe drop (2mm to 4mm):** Keeps your heel closer to the ground for a stable, athletic base.
- **Firm midsoles:** Minimal compression means when you squat or lift, your energy goes into the floor rather than being absorbed by squishy foam.
- **Lateral support:** Reinforced sidewalls and wraps prevent your feet from sliding off the footbed during side-to-side movements like speed skaters or side lunges.
- **Durable uppers:** Built with woven materials or guards to resist tears from rope climbs, burpees, and planks.
