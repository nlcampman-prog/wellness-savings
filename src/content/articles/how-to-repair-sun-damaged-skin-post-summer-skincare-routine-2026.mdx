## The Three Pillars of Post-Summer Skin Repair

Before we talk about products, we have to understand what UV light actually does to your skin structure. UV radiation generates a massive cascade of free radicals that damage your skin on three distinct levels:

1.  **Skin Barrier Disruption:** UV rays strip the natural lipids (ceramides and fatty acids) that keep your outer skin layer intact, leading to transepidermal water loss (TEWL). This is why your skin feels dry and flaky.
2.  **Melanin Overproduction:** To protect itself from radiation, your skin triggers melanocytes to produce melanin. This is what causes sunspots, melasma, and uneven patches.
3.  **Collagen Degradation:** UV rays activate enzymes called matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) that physically chop up your collagen and elastin fibers, leading to premature fine lines and loss of firmness.

To heal your skin, your autumn routine must focus on **barrier replenishment**, **antioxidant repair**, and **internal structural support**.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### How long does it take to repair sun-damaged skin?
With a consistent daily routine, you will begin to see surface hydration improve within **7 to 14 days**. Fading dark spots and rebuilding collagen takes longer—typically **8 to 12 weeks** of daily Vitamin C and collagen supplementation.

### Can I use retinol on sun-damaged skin?
Retinol is excellent for increasing cell turnover and reversing sun damage, but you should only introduce it *after* your skin barrier is fully hydrated and no longer flaking or irritated. Start with a gentle retinol alternative (like Bakuchiol) or use a low-percentage retinol 1-2 times a week at night.
