## What Is HRV (and Why Should You Care)?

Heart rate variability — HRV — measures the variation in time between each heartbeat. If your heart beats at 60 beats per minute, that doesn't mean it beats exactly once per second. It might beat at 0.95 seconds, then 1.05 seconds, then 0.98 seconds. Higher variability between beats is a sign that your nervous system is responsive and adaptable. Lower variability means your system is stressed and less able to adapt.

| HRV State | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **High HRV** (more variation between beats) | Your body is recovered, rested, and ready for stress. Your parasympathetic nervous system (rest-and-digest) is dominant. |
| **Low HRV** (steady, metronomic beats) | Your body is under stress — from training, illness, poor sleep, alcohol, or mental load. Your sympathetic nervous system (fight-or-flight) is dominant. |
| **HRV trend dropping** | You're accumulating stress faster than you're recovering. If this continues, you're heading toward overtraining or illness. |
| **HRV trend rising** | Your recovery is outpacing your stress. You're getting fitter. |

In the simplest terms: **high HRV = green light for hard training. Low HRV = red flag.** A single day of low HRV isn't concerning — life happens. But a multi-day downward trend is your body's way of tapping you on the shoulder and saying "ease up."

## Why HRV Is Different for Women

Most HRV research was conducted on men. But women's physiology introduces variables that change how HRV should be interpreted:

### 1. The Menstrual Cycle Affects HRV
HRV fluctuates across the menstrual cycle in predictable patterns. During the follicular phase (the first half, after your period), estrogen rises and HRV tends to be higher — your body is more resilient and recoverable. During the luteal phase (the second half, after ovulation), progesterone rises, and HRV tends to be lower — your body is under more baseline stress.

This means a drop in HRV during your luteal phase isn't necessarily a warning sign. It's expected physiology. A good HRV tracking practice for women accounts for these cyclical patterns rather than treating every dip as a problem.

### 2. Perimenopause and Menopause Change the Baseline
During perimenopause, hormonal fluctuations become less predictable, and HRV readings can become more volatile. This doesn't mean your training is wrong — it means your baseline is shifting. Tracking HRV through this transition helps you understand what's hormonal versus what's actual overtraining.

### 3. Mental Load Shows Up in HRV
Women carry disproportionately high mental loads — household management, childcare logistics, emotional labor — and that cognitive stress depresses HRV just as effectively as a hard workout does. If your HRV is low but your training volume hasn't changed, look at your life load: are you sleeping poorly? Managing a family crisis? Dreading something at work? HRV picks up on all of it.

## How to Use HRV to Optimize Your Training

Here's a practical framework for using HRV data in your training:

| HRV Signal | What to Do |
|---|---|
| **HRV at or above baseline** | Green light. Train hard. Your body is ready for intensity. |
| **HRV slightly below baseline (1 day)** | Yellow light. Train but consider scaling intensity or volume. Maybe swap heavy squats for tempo work. |
| **HRV significantly below baseline (2+ days)** | Red flag. Something is off. Prioritize sleep, hydration, and low-intensity movement (walking, mobility, yoga). Do not push through. |
| **HRV drops during luteal phase** | Expected. Don't panic. You can still train — but this is a great week for technique work, mobility, and moderate intensity rather than PR attempts. |
| **HRV rising steadily over weeks** | You're getting fitter. Your body is adapting positively to your training load. Keep doing what you're doing. |

The most common mistake: ignoring the red flags. It's tempting to "push through" a low-HRV day because your training plan says so. But HRV is a leading indicator — it tells you what's coming 1–3 days before you feel it. If you rest when HRV says rest, you'll avoid the 5-day crash. If you ignore it, the crash finds you.
