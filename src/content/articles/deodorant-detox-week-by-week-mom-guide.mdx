## What "detox" actually means (and what it does not)

Antiperspirant uses aluminum salts to form a gel plug in your sweat ducts. You sweat less. Bacteria have less moisture and fewer leftover proteins to feed on. Odor drops. That is the whole trick.

When you stop, three things happen at once:

1. **The plugs dissolve.** You sweat more for a while. Sweat itself has almost no smell. The smell comes from bacteria (especially *Corynebacterium*) eating the fats and proteins in that sweat.
2. **The microbiome shifts.** Years of aluminum, fragrance, and a higher underarm pH change which bacteria live there. When you stop plugging, the community rebalances. That can smell *different* — not always worse, but unfamiliar. Your brain files unfamiliar as "I stink."
3. **Your skin barrier may be angry.** Baking soda (in a lot of "natural" sticks) is alkaline. Underarm skin likes a slightly acidic pH. Swap aluminum for a baking-soda brick and you can get the red, peeling rash that makes people swear off clean deodorant forever.

None of that is a medical detox. You are not sweating out heavy metals. You are waiting for a body part to remember how to work without a cork in it.

## Week 1: louder sweat, weirder smell, do not quit on Wednesday

This is the week people abandon.

Your glands are unplugged. You will feel wetter. You might smell like onions, or metallic, or just *not you*. Shower. Exfoliate the underarm once (washcloth, not a face acid peel). Wear a black shirt to school pickup if you need a confidence costume.

**What I actually do in week one:**

- Apply clean deodorant to *clean, dry* skin. Damp pits plus powder formulas equal paste and stains.
- Reapply after a workout. That is not failure. Aluminum spoiled us.
- Wash shirts sooner. Old aluminum residue in the fabric plus new sweat is a smell sandwich.
- Start on a Thursday or Friday so the worst 72 hours are not a parent-teacher conference.

## Week 2: the fork in the road

Sweat volume usually calms down. Odor starts to tell you the truth about the product.

- If you smell fine until 2pm and then fall apart, you need a stronger mechanism (AHA like Kosas, or charcoal-magnesium like Schmidt's), not more essential oil.
- If you are red, itchy, or peeling, stop the soda. Humble sensitive or Lume. Give the skin three days. Then resume.
- If you smell fine but hate the scent, that is a shopping problem, not a biology problem. Native and Phlur exist for this exact mood.

This is also when people "fail" because they went to a spin class in a formula designed for a desk day. Match the stick to the day.

## Weeks 3–4: new normal, then be honest

By week three you should know:

1. Whether this formula lasts a school-plus-errands day.
2. Whether you need a "workout stick" and a "life stick."
3. Whether baking soda is banned in your bathroom forever.

You will still sweat. That is the point. You should not smell like you forgot deodorant. If you do, the formula is wrong — the detox is not "incomplete."

I keep two in rotation: a simple daily (Humble or Native) and a performance or whole-body option (Kosas or Lume). Schmidt's is the heavy-duty drawer stick. Phlur is the going-out stick. Salt & Stone is the one that looks like I have my life together even when I do not.

## How to not sabotage yourself

**Wash the shirts.** Aluminum plus sweat leaves yellow. New deodorant on an old yellow pit stain still smells like the old life.

**Exfoliate once a week, gently.** Dead skin holds odor. You do not need a peel. A washcloth counts.

**Do not layer three brands at 7am.** You will not decode which one failed.

**Give it 21 days or change one variable.** Product *or* routine, not both on the same Tuesday.

**Sensitive skin:** magnesium or mandelic acid. Not "natural fragrance" as a personality.

## FAQ

### How long does a deodorant detox last?
For most people, the dramatic phase is **7 to 14 days**. By **3 to 4 weeks** you know if the formula works. If you are still a wreck at day 30, switch products. The transition is done. The stick is not.

### Can I ease off aluminum instead of quitting overnight?
Yes. Some people use antiperspirant at night and clean deodorant in the morning for a week. I did cold turkey because I do not remember a two-step anything. Both are fine.

### Why do I smell worse than before?
Your nose is calibrated to "no sweat." You are sweating again. Also, some natural scents (patchouli, charcoal, heavy oils) smell louder than the floral chemical bouquet you used to wear. Try Native coconut-vanilla or Phlur Vanilla Skin if the hippie stick is the actual problem.
