## 1. Newborns (Birth to 3 Months): Round-the-Clock Nourishment

At this early stage, your baby’s stomach is tiny (about the size of a cherry on day one, growing to the size of an egg by month one). Their diet consists 100% of breast milk or infant formula.

### The Schedule:
*   **Feeding Frequency:** Every **2 to 3 hours** (breastfed babies) or **3 to 4 hours** (formula-fed babies).
*   **Total Daily Feeds:** 8 to 12 feeding sessions per 24 hours.
*   **Feeding Volume:** 2 to 4 ounces of breast milk or formula per feeding.
*   **Key Tip:** Do not follow a strict clock-based schedule yet. Feed your newborn **on demand** whenever they show early hunger cues (rooting, sucking on hands, lip-smacking). Never let a newborn sleep longer than 4 hours without a feed until they have regained their birth weight and received clearance from your pediatrician.

## 2. Infants (3 to 6 Months): Establishing a Predictable Rhythm

By 3 to 6 months, your baby is sleeping in longer stretches, and their stomach capacity has grown significantly, allowing them to drink more milk at each session and go longer between feedings.

### The Schedule:
*   **Feeding Frequency:** Every **3 to 4 hours**.
*   **Total Daily Feeds:** 5 to 7 feeding sessions per 24 hours.
*   **Feeding Volume:** 5 to 7 ounces of breast milk or formula per feeding.
*   **Key Tip:** This is the golden window where a highly predictable "Eat-Play-Sleep" routine naturally emerges. Feeding your baby immediately after they wake up from a nap ensures they have maximum energy to play, and prevents them from associating feeding with falling asleep, which supports healthy sleep training.

## 3. Older Infants (6 to 9 Months): Introducing Solids and Purees

At **6 months**, your baby reaches an incredibly exciting developmental milestone: **starting solid foods**. While breast milk or formula remains their primary source of nutrition, solids are introduced to help them practice chewing, swallowing, and to supply crucial micronutrients like iron and zinc.

## 4. Transitioning (9 to 12 Months): Moving Toward Table Foods

By 9 to 12 months, your baby is actively developing their "pincer grasp" (picking up small items with their thumb and index finger) and can transition from smooth purées to soft, textured finger foods.

### The Schedule:
*   **Milk Intake:** 3 to 4 milk feeds per day (roughly 16 to 24 ounces of breast milk or formula).
*   **Solids Intake:** **3 structured meals** of solids per day, plus 1 to 2 healthy snacks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if my baby is ready to start solid foods?
According to the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP), your baby is developmentally ready to start solid foods at around 6 months when they:
1.  **Can sit upright** in a highchair with minimal or no assistance and maintain good head control.
2.  **Show active interest** in your food (reaching for your plate, watching you chew).
3.  **Have lost the "extrusion reflex"** (automatically pushing food out of their mouth with their tongue).

### Should I offer water to my baby?
Before **6 months**, babies do not need water; breast milk and formula are 100% sufficient to keep them hydrated. Offering water early can fill their tiny stomachs and interfere with their ability to absorb nutrients from milk. Once they start solids at **6 months**, you can introduce **2 to 4 ounces of water** per day in a small open cup or sippy cup alongside their meals to help them practice drinking.
