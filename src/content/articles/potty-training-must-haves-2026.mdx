## What Makes Potty Training Gear Outstanding?

When selecting gear to help your toddler transition to the toilet, simplicity and physical safety should be your top priorities. Look for these critical features:

1.  **Ergonomic Stability:** Toddlers are naturally terrified of falling into the toilet. Any seat or step stool must be rock-solid, non-slip, and properly proportioned to make them feel safe.
2.  **Ease of Cleaning:** If a potty has too many nooks, crannies, or absorbent fabric parts, it will quickly harbor bacteria and smell. Look for seamless, wipe-clean designs.
3.  **Encouraging Independence:** The best gear allows your toddler to climb up, sit down, and wipe themselves with minimal adult help, fostering their confidence.

## My Top 5 Potty Training Tips from a Mom of 5

Over the years, I have gathered a few invaluable rules of thumb that will help save your sanity:

1.  **Wait For Readiness:** Don't start just because of a certain age or pressure from preschool. Look for active signs of readiness, like telling you when they are wet, staying dry for 2 hours, and wanting to pull their pants down.
2.  **Ditch the Digital Screens:** Bringing a tablet or phone into the bathroom to keep them seated can create a dependency where they won't sit without it. Stick to wooden toys or books instead.
3.  **Keep a Potty in the Car:** A collapsible travel potty with disposable liners is a complete lifesaver for road trips, park outings, or target runs when they suddenly yell *"I HAVE TO GO NOW!"*
4.  **Use a Reward Chart:** Keep a sticker reward chart directly on the bathroom wall. Let them place a sticker every time they sit, and a special double-sticker when they actually succeed.
5.  **Stay Calm and Clean On:** Accidents are 100% normal. Never get angry or show frustration. Simply say *"Oops! Pee goes in the potty. Let's clean it up together and try again next time."*

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### Should I buy a standalone floor potty or a toilet seat insert?
Both have their places! A standalone floor potty is fantastic for younger toddlers who are physically too small to climb onto the big toilet. However, a secure toilet seat insert like **Gafly** is much easier to clean (no dumping messy pots!), and makes transitioning to standard toilet usage seamless.

### How many pairs of training pants do I need?
In the first week of training, I recommend having at least **8 to 12 pairs of absorbent cotton training pants** on hand. This ensures you have plenty of clean pairs to rotate through while laundry is running.
