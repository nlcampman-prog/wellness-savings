## What Cortisol Actually Does (And Why It's Not All Bad)

First, let's be fair to cortisol. It's not the villain. Cortisol is a vital hormone that helps you wake up, helps you handle real danger, and helps regulate your blood sugar and immune system. You literally couldn't survive without it.

The problem isn't cortisol. It's *chronic* cortisol — the kind that never turns off because your body thinks you're under constant threat.

Here's the evolutionary logic. Your body doesn't know the difference between "a bear is chasing me" and "my inbox is overwhelming and I haven't had a break in three years." When it detects stress, it releases cortisol, which tells your body: *store energy, hold onto fat, this might be a famine.* Even more specifically, it tells your body to stash fat right around your middle, because that's the most accessible fuel depot for emergencies.

So when your stress never really shuts off, your cortisol stays elevated. And chronically elevated cortisol does a few really unhelpful things:

- **It promotes belly fat storage.** This is the "stress belly" everyone talks about — and it's real. High cortisol is strongly linked to visceral (belly) fat.
- **It drives cravings.** High cortisol spikes your blood sugar, and when it comes back down, you crash and crave sugar and carbs to re-raise it. It's a vicious cycle.
- **It wrecks your sleep.** High cortisol at night is the "tired but wired" feeling — you're exhausted but you can't switch off.
- **It slows your metabolism and promotes muscle breakdown.** Over time, it makes it harder to build muscle and easier to hold onto fat.

Sound familiar? If you're stressed and it feels like your metabolism has just... given up, this is a big reason why.

## The Cortisol–Insulin Connection Nobody Explains

Here's where it gets really interesting, and I wish someone had explained this to me years ago.

When you're chronically stressed, your body stays in a state where it's trying to keep your blood sugar up (to fuel the "emergency"). To do that, it keeps insulin higher too. And chronic high insulin — well, it's one of the main drivers of stubborn weight gain, especially around the middle.

This is why stress management isn't just "nice to have." It's a metabolic lever. When I finally got my stress under control, I noticed that my carb cravings dropped, my sleep improved, and things started moving again — not because I magically ate less, but because my hormones were finally on my side.
