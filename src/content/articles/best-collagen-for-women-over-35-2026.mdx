## Why Collagen Declines Faster for Women

Collagen production is tied to estrogen. As estrogen levels begin their slow decline in the mid-thirties and accelerate during perimenopause, collagen synthesis drops with them. Add the demands of pregnancy, breastfeeding, and the chronic sleep deprivation of raising kids, and your body's collagen reserves are being drawn down faster than they're replenished.

The visible signs — thinner skin, less "bounce," deeper expression lines — are what most women notice first. But the invisible signs matter more: joint cartilage thinning, reduced bone density, and a compromised gut lining. These are the reasons women over 35 report more joint pain, longer injury recovery, and more digestive issues than they did a decade earlier.

### Types of Collagen That Matter

Not all collagen types target the same tissue. A quality supplement for women over 35 should address all the types that decline with age:

| Collagen Type | What It Supports | Why It Matters After 35 |
|---|---|---|
| **Type I** | Skin, hair, nails, bones, tendons | The most abundant type. Directly addresses skin elasticity and fine lines. |
| **Type II** | Joint cartilage | Knees, hips, and hands feel the decline first. Type II targets joint comfort specifically. |
| **Type III** | Skin, blood vessels, organs | Works alongside Type I for skin structure. Also supports cardiovascular tissue. |
| **Type V** | Cell membranes, hair, placenta | Supports Type I and II fiber formation — a "helper" collagen. |
| **Type X** | Bone formation, cartilage | Critical for bone density maintenance as estrogen drops. |

A single-source collagen (bovine only, or marine only) typically covers Types I and III. A **multi-collagen** blend covers the full spectrum — I, II, III, V, and X — and is the better choice for women over 35 who want comprehensive support, not just skincare benefits.
