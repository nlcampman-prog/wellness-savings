## Running Stroller Anatomy: Critical Safety Features to Look For

Before we compare the top brands, let's talk about why specialized running strollers are built differently. Pushing a stroller while running alters your natural stride, so a high-quality jogging stroller utilizes several key biomechanical safety features:

1.  **Locking Front Swivel Wheel:** This is the single most critical safety feature. For slow walks, you want a swivel wheel for easy steering. But when running, **you must lock the front wheel in a fixed position**. If a front wheel swivels at high speed and hits a small pebble, it can pivot sharply, causing the entire stroller to flip over.
2.  **Pneumatic Air-Filled Tires:** Unlike standard hard-plastic wheels, jogging strollers utilize large, air-filled tires (usually 12-inch front and 16-inch rear) that act like shock absorbers, delivering an incredibly smooth ride for your passenger.
3.  **Active Suspension System:** High-end strollers feature physical coil or spring suspension systems that absorb impact, protecting your baby’s spine and brain from constant jostling.
4.  **Hand Brake and Wrist Tether:** A hand brake on the handlebar is vital for slowing down when heading down steep hills, while a wrist tether ensures the stroller cannot roll away if you accidentally lose your grip.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### What age can my baby start riding in a running stroller?
This is a vital safety guideline: **You should not run with your baby in a jogging stroller until they are at least 6 to 8 months old.** 

Before this age, your baby does not have the necessary neck strength and head control to support themselves against the physical vibrations of high-speed running. 

For infants under 6 months, you can use the stroller for slow, gentle walks by attaching an approved infant car seat (like the **Maxi-Cosi** system), but save the running for when they are older and have strong neck control.

### How often do I need to pump the tires?
Because jogging stroller tires are standard, air-filled bicycle tires, they will naturally lose air pressure over time. I highly recommend checking your tire pressure once every **2 to 3 weeks** and keeping a small, hand-held bicycle pump in your stroller's cargo basket to top them off before a run.
