## What to Look For in a Home Pilates Reformer

Before buying, it's important to understand that not all home reformers are built equal. Here are the key technical details you should consider:

- **Springs vs. Elastics/Cords:** Professional studio reformers use metal springs. They provide smooth, progressive resistance (tension increases as they stretch). Budget home reformers often use elastic bungee cords, which can feel "jerky" and lose tension over time. If you want a true studio feel, prioritize metal springs.
- **Frame Material (Wood vs. Metal):** Wood frames (usually maple or oak) look beautiful and feel like high-end furniture, but they are heavy and non-collapsible. Aluminum or steel frames are sleeker, lighter, and often fold or roll for easy storage.
- **Carriage Height:** Low-profile reformers sit close to the floor (around 5 to 9 inches). They are easier to store but harder to get on and off. Elevated reformers (14+ inches) are much easier for anyone with back issues or limited mobility but require more permanent space.
- **Tower Integration:** If you want to do Cadillac exercises (like hanging straps and spring towers), look for a reformer that is "tower-ready" so you can upgrade your setup later.
