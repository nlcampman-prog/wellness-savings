Let me tell you about the week I almost quit exercising entirely.

It was three months ago. I'd just finished a hard week of training — five workouts, two of them brutal — and my body was screaming. My knees ached when I went up the stairs. I was snapping at the kids over nothing. My sleep, which is already a precious commodity with five children in the house, had gone completely off the rails.

And what did I do? I booked another workout. Because somewhere along the way, I'd convinced myself that pushing through was strength, and resting was weakness.

Spoiler: I was wrong. And the week after that, when I finally let my body recover, was the week everything clicked.

If you're a mom who exercises — or who wants to — this is the honest conversation about recovery I wish someone had given me years ago.

## The Lie We Tell Ourselves About "Rest Days"

Here's the thing nobody says out loud: most moms don't skip rest days because they're lazy. We skip them because we're terrified of what happens if we stop.

Stop exercising and the weight comes back. Stop pushing and you lose your edge. Stop showing up and you've failed at the one thing that's just for you.

I get it. I've lived it. But here's the uncomfortable truth I learned the hard way: **rest is not the opposite of training. Rest is part of training.** Your muscles don't grow in the gym — they grow while you sleep, while you rest, while you let the damage repair.

That's not a wellness-Instagram platitude. It's physiology. When you lift or run, you create micro-tears in muscle tissue. Your body repairs those tears *between* workouts, and that repair is what makes you stronger. Skip the recovery, and you're not building — you're just accumulating damage until your body forces a stop, usually in the form of injury, illness, or burnout.

---

## What Actually Happened When I Pushed Too Hard

I want to be specific about what overtraining looked like for me, because I think a lot of moms will recognize it:

- **My sleep got worse, not better.** I was exhausted but wired — falling asleep was hard, and I'd wake up at 3 a.m. with my heart pounding.
- **My workouts got harder at the same weights.** Everything felt heavy. My pace was slower. I assumed I was getting weaker, so I pushed harder. Wrong move.
- **I was hungry all the time but never satisfied.** My cortisol was elevated, my recovery was shot, and my body was screaming for fuel it wasn't getting.
- **I stopped enjoying it.** The thing I used to love became a chore I dreaded.

If any of that sounds familiar, that's your nervous system telling you to back off. When you exercise while under-recovered, you're not training your muscles — you're training your stress response to stay on high alert. And for moms, who are already running on more stress than we admit, that's a recipe for a crash.

## What Actually Works: My Honest Recovery Reset

After that awful week, I rebuilt my recovery routine from scratch. Not from a fitness magazine — from what my body was actually telling me. Here's what made the difference:

### 1. I Stopped Treating Sleep Like a Luxury

This is the big one. Sleep is when your body releases growth hormone, repairs muscle tissue, and clears the metabolic waste from hard training. You can take every supplement on the shelf, but if you're sleeping five hours a night, you're leaving most of your recovery on the table.

I'm a mom of five — I don't get to just "sleep more." What I *can* do is protect the sleep I get. I moved my workouts earlier so I wasn't wired at bedtime. I stopped scrolling in bed. And I looked honestly at what was keeping me from deep sleep.

If you struggle with the "tired but wired" cycle — and so many moms do — I wrote up everything I've learned about [magnesium for sleep and recovery](/articles/magnesium-sleep-recovery-busy-moms-guide-2026) in my busy mom's guide. It's the single most underrated recovery tool I've found, and it's cheap.

### 2. I Made Recovery a "Gym Session" in My Calendar

Recovery doesn't happen by accident. It happens when you schedule it like you schedule everything else — with the same commitment you give a workout.

For me that looks like: ten minutes of stretching and foam rolling after every workout, not when I "have time" but as a non-negotiable part of the session. I keep a [foam roller](/articles/best-foam-roller-for-muscle-recovery-2026) by the couch, not in a closet, so it's actually possible to use it while the kids watch a show.

Sounds small. It changed everything about how my legs feel the next day.

### 3. I Stopped Working Out Hungry and Under-Fueled

I used to exercise fasted because it felt "efficient" — and then wonder why I felt terrible afterward. Your body needs fuel to repair. If you train hard and then eat like you didn't train, you're shortchanging the entire process.

I'm not going to lecture you with macros. I will say this: after a hard workout, your body wants protein and carbohydrates, in that order of priority. If you're a mom who works out, my guide to the [best supplements for workout recovery](/articles/best-supplements-for-workout-recovery-2026) breaks down what's worth taking and what's a waste of money — including the timing that actually matters.

### 4. I Learned What "Active Recovery" Really Means

Rest doesn't have to mean lying on the couch (though sometimes it absolutely should). Active recovery — walking, gentle yoga, stretching — keeps blood flowing to recovering muscles without adding stress. It's the difference between sitting still with sore legs and actually helping them heal.

## How to Know You Need a Recovery Day (Before Your Body Forces One)

You don't have to wait until you're injured or burned out to rest. Here are the signs I now watch for:

| Sign | What It Means |
|---|---|
| Sleep quality drops for no obvious reason | Your nervous system is overloaded |
| Resting heart rate is up | You're carrying more stress than you're recovering from |
| Same weights feel heavy | Your muscles haven't finished repairing |
| You're irritable, especially with your kids | Cortisol is elevated; you need a downshift |
| You're dreading workouts | Training has become stress, not strength |

If you check two or more of those boxes, take a recovery day. Not a "I'll just do a light workout" day — an actual rest day. The world will not end. Your fitness will not collapse. I promise you, the opposite happens: you come back stronger.

## The Bottom Line

Here's what I want you to take from this: **recovery is not a reward you earn. It's a requirement you meet.**

You don't get a medal for pushing through exhaustion. You get a medal for training smart enough to do it again tomorrow. And as a mom, you're already running on fumes half the time — the last thing you need is to treat your body like it doesn't need maintenance.

So take the rest day. Stretch after your workout. Eat something after you train. Sleep as much as your life allows, and be gentle with yourself about the rest.

Your body is doing an incredible thing, every single day. It deserves better than being run into the ground. It deserves the same care you give everyone else in your life.

And honestly? You do too.
