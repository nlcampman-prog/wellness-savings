## Why "just drink more water" backfires after three coffees

Coffee is a diuretic for some people, mildly. The bigger issue in my house is **I replace breakfast fluids with caffeine and then chug water at 11am**. Water without sodium can leave you feeling puffy, headachy, and weirdly thirsty. The clinical word people throw around is hyponatremia. The mom word is "I feel hungover and I did not even go out."

Signs I actually wait for before I start squeezing drops into everything:

- headache that water does not touch,
- salt cravings that are not just "I walked past pretzels,"
- muscle twinges after a workout that was not even impressive,
- brain fog that improves after a salty meal more than after another latte.

If you have those and you also live on coffee, try electrolytes for a week before you buy a new nootropic.

## What I do not do

- **Sports drinks with a candy aisle of sugar** for a 25-minute walk.
- **Salt tablets** I saw on a podcast, unsupervised.
- **Gallon challenges.** That is a bladder sport.

Eat. A little sodium at breakfast (eggs, not just oat milk) does more than a sad liter of plain water after three americanos.

If your greens powder is also your "hydration," it is not. [Greens are not lunch](/articles/do-you-need-a-greens-powder-or-lunch/) and they are not electrolytes either.

## FAQ

### Can I put Buoy in mushroom coffee?
Yes. It is still coffee-ish liquid. I have done it. Taste is fine if you do not use a novel's worth of drops.

### How do I know it is working?
Headache eases. You stop feeling like you ran a marathon in a parking lot. If nothing changes in a week, the issue might be sleep, iron, or the fact that you are only eating crackers. Electrolytes are not a plot twist for every problem.

### Is this the same as LMNT?
Same idea (sodium-forward), different format. Buoy is drops. Thorne is a tub. I care that you will use it.

### POTS or pregnancy?
Those are clinician conversations. I mention POTS in the [Buoy review](/articles/buoy-hydration-review-2026/) because readers ask. I do not diagnose you from a blog.
