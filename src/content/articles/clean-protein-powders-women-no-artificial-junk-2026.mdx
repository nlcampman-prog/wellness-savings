## What "Clean Protein" Actually Means

"Clean" isn't a regulated term, so brands throw it around freely. Here's what I look for:

| What "Clean" Should Mean | What to Avoid |
|---|---|
| **Short ingredient list** (under 10 ingredients) | Artificial sweeteners (sucralose, aspartame, acesulfame potassium) |
| **Grass-fed, pasture-raised, or organic sourcing** | "Natural flavors" (a catch-all that can hide dozens of chemicals) |
| **Third-party tested** for heavy metals and contaminants | Soy protein isolate (highly processed, often GMO) |
| **No artificial sweeteners** — stevia or monk fruit only | Maltodextrin, dextrose, and other fillers |
| **Digestible** — no bloating or GI distress | Carrageenan, xanthan gum in excess |

A clean protein powder should do one thing: provide high-quality protein without introducing junk your body has to process. Bonus points if it actually tastes good.

## What About Plant-Based Proteins?

You'll notice I didn't include a dedicated plant-based option. Here's why: most plant proteins (pea, rice, hemp) have incomplete amino acid profiles and require blending multiple sources to match the bioavailability of animal proteins. If you're strictly plant-based, look for a blend that combines pea and rice protein — that combination provides a complete amino acid profile.

For everyone else, the three options above — beef isolate, whey-colla blend, and whey isolate — provide superior amino acid profiles with minimal processing.
