## What "squat-proof" actually means

Squat-proof means the fabric stays **opaque when it is stretched to its thinnest**. A standing selfie is not a test. The crotch and the back of the thighs are where cheap nylon gives up.

Three things make sheerness worse:

1. **Light colors.** Gray, mauve, pale olive. Black and navy forgive more.
2. **Thin "naked feel" fabric.** Buttery is lovely. Buttery in sunlight can be a window.
3. **A size too small.** Overstretching turns a decent weave into mesh.

Compression is not the same as coverage. You can have a vice-grip waistband and still flash the world when you lunge.

## The bathroom test (do this in order)

Wear the underwear you would actually wear to the gym or Target. Thong plus thin leggings is a different movie than boyshorts.

**1. The overhead-light squat.**  
Stand under the brightest light you have. Feet hip-width. Sit into a full squat like you are picking up a toddler. Hold for three seconds. Look in the mirror *and* take a photo from behind if you live with someone you can bribe. If you can see underwear lines or skin tone through the fabric, they are not squat-proof.

**2. The toe-touch.**  
Hinge forward. This stretches the back of the thighs, which is where "they were fine in the squat" pairs often fail.

**3. The lunge.**  
One foot forward, back knee toward the floor. Inner thigh and crotch get pulled on a diagonal. If they pass squat and fail lunge, they will fail playground.

**4. The window test.**  
Daylight is meaner than bedroom bulbs. If you can, stand in front of a window for the squat. Fluorescent grocery lighting is even meaner. If they pass a window, they will probably survive Costco.

**5. The sit-down.**  
Sit on a hard chair or the closed toilet lid. Cheap fabric goes shiny and thin across the seat. You will sit more than you squat this week. Test it.

If any of those five shows skin, underwear, or a color change to "foggy," return them. You are not being extra. You are being a person who bends over in public.

## Color and care count more than the brand name

I have had expensive pairs go sheer in lavender and cheap pairs stay solid in black. **Buy dark first.** Test the fun color after you trust the fabric.

Care is how squat-proof dies:

- Wash cold, skip fabric softener (it coats stretch fibers).
- Hang dry. A dryer is how buttery fabric becomes tissue.
- Do not yank them on wet. That overstretches the gusset.

A pair that passed in March can fail in October if you cooked the elastane. I wrote the full laundry routine — cold, no softener, hang dry, and the one soak that saves a sour pair — in [how to wash workout leggings so they last](/articles/how-to-wash-workout-leggings-so-they-last/).

## FAQ

### Why do my leggings look fine standing up and fail in a squat?
Standing does not stretch the fabric to its thinnest. The squat, lunge, and toe-touch do. Always test stretched.

### Can lining in the front fake squat-proof?
A gusset lining helps comfort. It does not make the back of the thigh opaque. Test from behind.

### Are thicker leggings always safer?
Usually, until they are cheap thick polyester that pills and still shines. Density plus a tight knit matters more than "feels heavy."

### I already own sheer pairs. Can I only wear them at home?
Yes. That is a valid category. I have house-only leggings. I do not take them to Costco.
